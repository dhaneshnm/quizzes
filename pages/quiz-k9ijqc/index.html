  1. Question 1 of 10 According to the CRISPR Democracy authors, what did the Asilomar scientists do first in shaping governance?

    The authors say questions were narrowed to risks scientists know best, so publics had to defer.

  2. Question 2 of 10 Which principle did the Asilomar scientists adopt to address the risk of dangerous organisms escaping?

    They adopted containment, physical and biological controls to keep organisms enclosed.

  3. Question 3 of 10 What does the article say was in shorter supply at Asilomar?

    Intelligence and expertise were abundant, but diversity of viewpoints was lacking.

  4. Question 4 of 10 Which topics were explicitly excluded from the Asilomar agenda, per the article?

    The article states biosecurity and ethics questions were explicitly excluded.

  5. Question 5 of 10 How many keys does each Clipper Chip device have under the White House plan?

    Each device has two keys deposited separately in two key-escrow databases.

  6. Question 6 of 10 Who would be able to access the escrowed keys under the Clipper plan?

    Access was limited to officials with legal authorization for a wiretap.

  7. Question 7 of 10 What did the White House say about the Clipper Chip and law enforcement authority?

    The statement says the technology gives law enforcement no new authorities.

  8. Question 8 of 10 What did the Future of Life Institute letter call on AI labs to do?

    The letter asks for a pause of at least six months on training beyond GPT-4.

  9. Question 9 of 10 What does the letter say should happen if a pause cannot be enacted quickly?

    The letter says governments should institute a moratorium.

  10. Question 10 of 10 According to the letter, who must not be delegated decisions about AI's effects on society?

    The letter says such decisions must not be delegated to unelected tech leaders.
