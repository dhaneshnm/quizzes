  1. Question 1 of 10 Who coined the Collingridge dilemma, and in what work?

    The idea was coined by David Collingridge in his 1980 book.

  2. Question 2 of 10 What is the 'power problem' in the Collingridge dilemma?

    The power problem is that control or change is hard once technology is entrenched; the information problem is about prediction.

  3. Question 3 of 10 What was Collingridge's own proposed solution?

    The article says his solution was not exactly the precautionary principle but Intelligent Trial and Error.

  4. Question 4 of 10 Why did early toxicity testing initially fail to explain the decline of birds of prey?

    Extensive tests on adult bird mortality found no relationship, making toxicity seem implausible, though the real impairment was reproduction.

  5. Question 5 of 10 What happened after DDT was banned from most US uses in 1972?

    The recovery is described as very strong evidence that DDT was the true cause.

  6. Question 6 of 10 What does Candice Odgers say about studies linking social media to poor mental health?

    Odgers argues the research shows only correlation and mostly cannot separate cause from effect.

  7. Question 7 of 10 On what do Haidt and Odgers agree about social media?

    Both acknowledge potential harms and agree tech companies need to protect young users better.

  8. Question 8 of 10 According to the Wingspread Statement, who should bear the burden of proof?

    The statement says the proponent, rather than the public, should bear the burden of proof.

  9. Question 9 of 10 According to the Wingspread Statement, when should precautionary measures be taken?

    The principle calls for precaution when an activity raises threats of harm, even without full scientific proof.

  10. Question 10 of 10 What do the Schwartz Reisman authors say about the policy window for AI companions?

    They argue governments still have an opportunity to set guardrails before these systems become deeply embedded.
