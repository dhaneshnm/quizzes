  1. Question 1 of 9 According to the Collingridge dilemma, what is the 'information problem'?

    The information problem is that impacts are hard to predict until a technology is extensively developed and widely used.

  2. Question 2 of 9 What is the 'power problem' in the Collingridge dilemma?

    The power problem is that control or change becomes difficult once a technology has become entrenched.

  3. Question 3 of 9 How does the article describe the Collingridge dilemma overall?

    The article calls it a methodological quandary in which efforts to control technology face a double-bind problem.

  4. Question 4 of 9 What did both Haidt and Odgers agree on at the end of their discussion?

    Despite their differences, both said more research is needed.

  5. Question 5 of 9 What does Odgers say about studies linking social media to mental health problems?

    Odgers argues the research shows only correlation, and that 90% of studies cannot separate cause from effect.

  6. Question 6 of 9 What is Haidt's position on social media and teen mental health?

    Haidt believes social media is directly harming teen mental health.

  7. Question 7 of 9 Under the Wingspread Statement, who should bear the burden of proof?

    The statement says the proponent of an activity, rather than the public, should bear the burden of proof.

  8. Question 8 of 9 According to the Schwartz Reisman Institute article, what is the state of the policy window for AI companion bots?

    The authors argue governments still have time to set guardrails before the systems become deeply embedded in daily life.

  9. Question 9 of 9 What early warning sign of digital addiction does the article cite about Character.ai?

    Early reports suggest companion interactions on Character.ai last 4 times as long as ChatGPT ones, averaging 2 hours a day.
